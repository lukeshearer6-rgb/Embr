# R6: CAP Code checklist for Embr launch marketing claims
Question: Which CAP Code (UK non-broadcast) rules apply to Embr's launch claims on the website, landing page, Instagram and email, and how should the marketer check each piece of copy?
Answer in short: Every objective claim ("0% of your earnings", "paid instantly") needs documentary evidence held before it runs (3.7). "Free" is only allowed if talent pay nothing beyond unavoidable cost, and any later paid tier must be made clear (3.23, 3.24). "Book now, pay later" is a credit-type claim: CAP has dedicated guidance and the regulatory side is unverified, so it should not appear in public copy until the product exists and a specialist has reviewed it. This is a research checklist, not legal advice.

All pages checked 2026-11-25. Rule wording below is quoted or closely fragmented from the ASA pages (the fetch tool returned fragments, so check the live page before quoting externally).

## Findings

### 1. Misleading advertising and substantiation (section 3)
- 3.1: "Marketing communications must not materially mislead or be likely to do so." [CAP Code s3](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html), checked 2026-11-25
- 3.3: "Marketing communications must not omit material information or information required to be included by law." Same source.
- 3.7: "Before distributing or submitting a marketing communication for publication, marketers must hold documentary evidence to prove claims that consumers are likely to regard as objective and that are capable of objective substantiation." Same source.
- 3.9 / 3.10: must not mislead "by omitting significant limitations and qualifications"; qualifications "must not mislead by not being presented clearly." Same source.
- 3.11: "must not mislead consumers by exaggerating the capability or performance of a product." Same source.
- 3.12: must not present legal consumer rights "as a distinctive feature". Same source.
- ASA advice: the ASA judges likely consumer interpretation, "rather than the marketer's intention"; testimonials alone "are unlikely to be considered sufficient to substantiate objective claims"; obvious puffery and clear opinion generally need no evidence. [Substantiation advice](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/substantiation.html), checked 2026-11-25. Also "In the absence of sufficient evidence, the ASA is likely to consider objective claims to be misleading." [Misleading advertising advice](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/misleading-advertising.html), checked 2026-11-25.
- Law behind the Code: Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, s.226 "Misleading actions" (includes "the provision of false or misleading information relating to a product, a trader" and "an overall presentation which is likely to deceive the average consumer"). [DMCC Act 2024 s.226](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/section/226), checked 2026-11-25. Only the extracted wording was read; full section not reviewed.

### 2. Prices, fees and "free" (3.17-3.26)
- 3.17: "Price statements must not mislead by omission, undue emphasis or distortion." 3.18: "Quoted prices must include non-optional taxes, duties, fees and charges that apply to all or most buyers." 3.19: if a fee "cannot be calculated in advance," say it is excluded and "state how it is calculated." 3.21: do not mislead "by failing to make clear the extent of the commitment the consumer must make to obtain the advertised price." 3.22: "up to" and "from" claims must not exaggerate benefits likely to be obtained. [CAP Code s3](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html), checked 2026-11-25
- Law: DMCC Act 2024 s.230 "Omission of material information from invitation to purchase": the total price must include "any fees, taxes, charges or other payments" the consumer will necessarily incur. [s.230](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/section/230), checked 2026-11-25
- 3.23: must not describe a product as "free", "gratis", "without charge" or similar "if the consumer has to pay anything other than the unavoidable cost of responding and collecting or paying for delivery." 3.24: "must make clear the extent of the commitment the consumer must make to take advantage of a 'free' offer." 3.25: must not call an element of a package "free" if "included in the package price" unless recently added "without increasing its price." 3.26: no "free trial" for money-back or non-refundable-purchase offers. [CAP Code s3](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html), checked 2026-11-25
- ASA "free" advice: free trial ads should say whether a paid subscription starts automatically and the financial commitment; services included in a package price generally cannot be called free; Quintain Living (2024) "FREE SUPERFAST WIFI" upheld because it was in the package cost; rule of thumb to avoid "free" for elements part of a package for more than six months. [Use of "free"](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/use-of-free.html), checked 2026-11-25
- Not found: an ASA page on price claims for marketplace commission or fee structures. Page https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/pricing-advertising-guidance.html did not load. Unverified: needs checking.

### 3. Comparisons and denigration (3.33-3.43)
Numbering note: in the current page the denigration rule is **3.41**; **3.42** is "must not take unfair advantage of the reputation of a competitor's trade mark, trade name or other distinguishing mark". Briefing asked for 3.42 as denigration; the live page shows otherwise. [CAP Code s3](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html), checked 2026-11-25
- 3.33: compare products "meeting the same needs or intended for the same purpose." 3.34: compare "objectively" "one or more material, relevant, verifiable and representative feature". 3.37: unidentified-competitor comparisons must not mislead and elements must not be "selected to give the marketer an unrepresentative advantage." 3.38: make the basis of a price comparison clear. 3.39: no falsely claimed price advantage.
- 3.41: comparisons with an identifiable competitor "must not discredit or denigrate another product, marketer, trade mark, trade name or other distinguishing mark."
- ASA on "best/leading": needs evidence covering the competitors compared (Verisure ruling example). [Substantiation advice](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/substantiation.html), checked 2026-11-25. A comparison page (comparative-advertising.html) did not load: unverified.

### 5. Testimonials and reviews (founding talent quotes)
Numbering note: in the current page these are 3.44 to 3.50 (not 3.45 to 3.52 as briefed). [CAP Code s3](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html), checked 2026-11-25
- 3.44: "Marketing communications must not contain fake consumer reviews." 3.45: "must make clear where consumer reviews have been incentivised." 3.46: no publishing reviews "in a misleading way", e.g. removing negative ones while publishing positive ones.
- 3.47: "must hold documentary evidence that a testimonial or endorsement ... is genuine ... and hold contact details for the person." 3.48: "Testimonials must relate to the advertised product." 3.49: factual claims in a testimonial "must not mislead". 3.50: "must not feature a testimonial without permission."
- 3.51 (CAP advice/ASA endorsement): must not "imply endorsement by the ASA or CAP." 3.52: no trust mark or approval claim without authorisation.
- ASA advice: keep documentary proof and permission; do not edit or take out of context; claims within a testimonial still need support; incentives and financial ties must be disclosed. Free webmail alone is weak evidence. [Testimonials and endorsements](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/testimonials-and-endorsements.html), checked 2026-11-25. The page does not directly address incentivised testimonials; rule 3.45 does.

### 6. Recognisability on Luke's Instagram (section 2)
- 2.1: "Marketing communications must be obviously identifiable as such." 2.3 says marketing communications must not falsely claim or imply the marketer is acting as a consumer, and must make clear their commercial intent where that is not apparent from the context (paraphrased). 2.2 covers unsolicited email. [CAP Code s2](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/02.html), checked 2026-11-25. (Quotes returned as fragments.)
- ASA: a brand posting on its own named account is generally clearly advertising; for founders or owners promoting their own brand on a personal account, if not immediately clear the ASA is likely to expect at least a prominent upfront "#Ad"-style label, visible before people engage; #myownbrand-style tags in corners were not sufficient. [Recognising ads: Brand-owned and paid social media](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/recognising-ads-brand-owned-and-paid-social-media.html), checked 2026-11-25
- Search also surfaced an influencer guide and a general social media page; not opened: [Recognising ads: Social media and influencer marketing](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/recognising-ads-social-media.html).
- Embr is not incorporated; the trader identity (3.5, "must not materially mislead by omitting the identity of the marketer") should be clear once a legal entity exists.

### 7. Marketing emails and data (section 10), brief
- Section 10 covers only "data used for direct marketing purposes". 10.1: "Marketers must not make persistent and unwanted marketing communications by any means." 10.2 to 10.4: give consumers identity, purposes and rights at collection, and handle data from other sources and new purposes. The page notes the section is under review following the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. Email-specific rules are 10.6 and 10.7 per the page (wording not read: unverified). [CAP Code s10](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/10.html), checked 2026-11-25
- 2.2: unsolicited email marketing must be "obviously identifiable as marketing communications". PECR and UK GDPR are outside this brief: unverified, not researched.
- Emailing real people is an outward action needing Luke's approval (CLAUDE.md).

## What this means for Embr
- Hold an evidence file before any copy goes live: fee schedule, pilot terms, test payout timings, signed testimonial permission.
- Replace "paid instantly" and "no commission" with precise, evidenced wording that says who pays what.
- Hold "book now, pay later" back from public copy until built and reviewed (BPP clinic enquiry may be a route; Luke decides what to ask).
- Label Luke's own promotional Instagram content upfront.
- Keep agency-comparison lines factual, or leave them out.
- Pricing, earnings and comparative claims in public copy need Luke's approval anyway (CLAUDE.md).
