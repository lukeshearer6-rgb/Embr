# Embr: talent subscription and the Employment Agencies Act (notes, 17 Nov 2026)

**Luke's position (17 Nov 2026):** Embr is not an agency. It's for unsigned models, new faces, talent who've left representation, and creatives generally. The subscription pays for what the app does for talent (usage tracking and handling, payment handling and protection, travel expenses, being paid on time), not for obtaining work.

## What the law says (not legal advice)
- The Employment Agencies Act 1973 defines an employment agency as a business providing services "for the purpose of finding persons employment". "Employment" includes contracts for services, so self-employed talent are covered.
- Agencies can't charge work-seekers for work-finding, with exceptions for entertainment and modelling. Even within the exception, photographic and fashion models can't be charged upfront fees, including fees for being listed on a website. Agencies also can't make work-finding conditional on buying other services.
- Fees charged to hirers (brands) are not regulated by the Act.

## Key cases
- **Equity v Talent Systems Europe (Spotlight), High Court 2025:** a subscription directory for performers was held *not* to be an employment agency. It was treated as a marketing tool, like Checkatrade, Yell or LinkedIn. Automated job emails and other extras weren't treated as work-finding. **Supports Luke's position.**
- **Simply Learning Tutor Agency v BEIS, High Court 2020:** online tutor-introduction platforms were held *to be* employment agencies, even though the tutors were self-employed. The court called the definition wide: "all arrangements through which a business supplies people personally to perform work".

## Where Embr sits
Embr does more than Spotlight's directory: job posts, smart matches, offers, bookings and payment handling. That pushes it towards the tutor-platform end. Labels don't decide status; the operating model does.

## Recommended guardrails
1. Get a short written opinion from an employment-agency specialist before charging talent subscriptions.
2. Terms and in-app copy: the subscription is software (usage, payments, travel, statements, profile tools). Avoid "join to get work".
3. Keep brand fees as the main transactional revenue; these are outside the Act's fee rules.
4. Review the "Smart matches" and "Jobs that match you" features with counsel.
